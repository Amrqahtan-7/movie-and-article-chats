# tell me about the war in yemen

*Status: VERIFIED (confirmed against sources)*

The war in Yemen, which began in 2014, is a complex and multifaceted conflict that has devastated the country and its people. The conflict started when Houthi insurgents, a militant Shiite Muslim group backed by Iran, took control of Yemen's capital and largest city, Sanaa, demanding lower fuel prices and a new government. After failed negotiations, the Houthis seized the presidential palace in January 2015, leading President Abd Rabbu Mansour Hadi and his government to resign.

In March 2015, a coalition of Gulf states led by Saudi Arabia launched a campaign of air strikes against the Houthi insurgents, with US logistical and intelligence support. The conflict has since become a proxy war between Iran and Saudi Arabia, with the Houthis controlling most of northwestern Yemen. The war has also led to the splintering of the economy into two broad economic zones under territories controlled by the Houthis and the Saudi-backed government. The economic crisis has compounded the ongoing humanitarian crisis, with a sharp depreciation of Yemen's currency in government-controlled areas significantly reducing people's purchasing power and pushing basic necessities out of reach.

The conflict has resulted in widespread humanitarian suffering and significant infrastructure damage in Yemen, long the Arab world's poorest country. Over 18 million people, more than half the population, need support, including 4.5 million people who have been displaced. The war has also led to the implementation of blockades, which have led to food insecurity and a psychological crisis in Yemen. The situation in Yemen has been labelled the world's worst humanitarian crisis, with millions forced from their homes due to years of conflict, poverty, and climate shocks.

The conflict has also led to the rise of al-Qaeda in the Arabian Peninsula (AQAP) in the south and east of the country, which has threatened the government's control. The Houthis have also reasserted control in the north and can threaten Israel, while the conflict has disrupted flows through the Red Sea, making it a more important conduit for energy and goods. The war has also limited humanitarian access and affected aid delivery, with the World Food Programme (WFP) terminating its operations in Houthi-controlled northern Yemen due to security risks and funding cuts.
