**Pakistan's Urgent Need for Job Creation**

Pakistan faces a daunting challenge in creating millions of jobs over the next decade to turn its youth bulge into an economic dividend. According to World Bank President Ajay Banga, the country needs to generate 2.5 million to 3 million jobs per year, which translates to roughly 25 to 30 million jobs over the next decade. Failure to do so could lead to instability, outward migration, or even domestic instability.

The World Bank has committed $4 billion per year to Pakistan to support job creation efforts. Agriculture is seen as a potential sector to create one-third of the jobs needed. However, the most urgent near-term need is the reform of the power sector. Banga emphasized that job creation is the top priority, calling it the "North Star" of the country's economic development. He noted that the World Bank is shifting its focus from individual projects to achieving tangible outcomes, and job creation is at the forefront of this effort.

Pakistan's universities have been ranked globally in terms of their research publications. According to a study published in the Pakistan Journal of Medical Sciences, the country's performance in the global science impact factor race has been improving. However, the focus on job creation remains a pressing concern, with the World Bank warning that the country needs to create around 30 million new jobs in the next decade to avoid instability and economic stagnation.
